Concept Explanation

Model Answer

Fine-tuning is the process of adapting a pre-trained foundation model to a specific downstream task or domain by further training it on a smaller, targeted dataset. Rather than training a model from scratch—which requires massive computational resources and vast amounts of data—fine-tuning leverages the general knowledge, patterns, and linguistic structures the model acquired during its initial pre-training phase. By updating the model's weights using a specialized dataset, the model learns to optimize its output for a particular objective, such as medical diagnosis, legal analysis, or a specific corporate tone. This allows the model to narrow its focus and significantly improve accuracy and relevance for niche applications. Depending on the implementation, this can involve updating all model parameters (full fine-tuning) or utilizing parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) techniques, which update only a small subset of weights to reduce computational overhead while maintaining high performance.
